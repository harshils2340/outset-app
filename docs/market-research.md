# Outset market research

*Compiled September 2026. Sources are linked inline. Where a number is derived (for example an average order value computed from disclosed gross bookings and booking counts) it is marked as such. Third-party blog figures for private companies' fees are labelled as reported, not confirmed.*

Outset in one line: an Airbnb-style guest site over a crawled catalog of about 12,400 US and Canadian experience operators (jet skis, kayaks, charters, cruises, skydiving, helicopters, balloons, parasailing, go-karts, escape rooms, axe throwing, paintball, horseback), instant booking, an Uber Eats-style operator dashboard, and a per-listing assistant (Otto) that answers only from the operator's own published information. Roughly 10,900 of the 12,400 operators take bookings by phone with no software; about 1,400 use FareHarbor; a few hundred use Peek, Xola, Rezdy, Bookeo or Checkfront.

---

## 1. Market size and structure

**Global.** Arival and Phocuswright size tours, activities and attractions at **$271 billion in 2025, heading to $342 billion by 2029**, an 8% CAGR versus about 5% for travel overall ([Arival](https://arival.travel/article/experiences-surging-towards-342-billion/), [Phocuswright](https://www.phocuswright.com/Travel-Research/Research-Updates/2026/tours-and-activities-are-outgrowing-travel-overall)). Only **33% of gross bookings were made online in 2025**, against 64% for travel as a whole; online was 17% in 2019 and is projected at 42% by 2029. OTAs are the fastest-growing channel, forecast to grow gross bookings more than fivefold from 2019 to 2029.

**US and Canada.** Arival does not publish a free North America line, but its regional reports and the size of the US players give a fair proxy. Viator alone booked $4.7 billion of gross booking value in 2025 across 22.9 million bookings, with North America its largest market ([Tripadvisor FY2025 results](https://ir.tripadvisor.com/static-files/aa7fa441-63ca-4330-b1cb-20d4a2498b8f), [PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/tripadvisor-q4-2025-earnings)). GetYourGuide says North America GMV grew 34% year over year in H1 2026, faster than its 27% global rate, with sightseeing and boat tours the top categories for first-time North American bookers ([GetYourGuide press](https://www.getyourguide.press/blog/summer2026momentum)). A reasonable working estimate for the US plus Canada is 25 to 30% of the global figure, so about **$65 to 80 billion in gross bookings**, of which the outdoor and adventure categories Outset targets (water sports, air sports, charters, cruises, motorsports, indoor entertainment) are a minority slice, plausibly $10 to 20 billion. Treat that as an estimate, not a citation.

**Structure: fragmented and offline.**
- More than 70% of operators are small or micro businesses; the supply base "has never consolidated the way air, hotel and cruise did" ([Arival](https://arival.travel/article/experiences-surging-towards-342-billion/)).
- Almost 2 in 5 operators globally have no reservation system. Large operators (50,000+ guests a year) are at 82% booking system adoption; small operators (under 1,000 guests) are at 42%. Nearly 80% of operators serve at most 10,000 guests a year ([Arival, "Tours and activities' dirty secret"](https://arival.travel/article/tours-and-activities-dirty-secret-many-operators-dont-use-tech/), [WiT](https://www.webintravel.com/gap-in-technology-adoption-in-experiences-sector-many-operators-lack-modern-booking-and-ticketing-systems/)). Outset's own crawl (about 88% of 12,400 operators with no detectable booking software) is consistent with this for the small outdoor operator segment, which skews even more offline than city tours.
- Operator survey data (5,664 qualified responses, Aug to Nov 2025): OTAs took **37% of bookings in 2025**, up from 33% in 2024 and 28% in 2023; operator-website direct bookings fell from 29% to 25%; online share overall was flat at about 60% for surveyed operators, meaning the shift is from direct to reseller, not from offline to online ([Arival](https://arival.travel/article/direct-bookings-dive-otas-rise/), [PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/otas-increased-booking-share-experiences-arival-report)). Note the survey over-represents tech-using operators; the gross-booking figure of 33% online is the better whole-market number.
- Phone-first businesses miss the phone. Multiple vendor studies converge on **about 60 to 62% of calls to small businesses going unanswered**, with roughly 85% of those callers not calling back ([411 Locals via SkipCalls](https://skipcalls.com/blog/percentage-business-calls-unanswered-statistics-2026), [Hicira compilation](https://www.hicira.com/missed-call-statistics)). These are vendor-published numbers selling call-answering products, so discount them, but the direction is right and it is the sharpest pitch to a phone-only operator.

**Take rates.** Viator runs a "mid-20s" take rate (revenue $924M on $4.7B GBV is 19.7% net of discounts and partner payouts, the mid-20s figure is the gross commission) ([Tripadvisor](https://ir.tripadvisor.com/static-files/aa7fa441-63ca-4330-b1cb-20d4a2498b8f)). GetYourGuide defaults to 30% for new operators, 25 to 28% negotiated ([OTA Playbook](https://otaplaybook.com/getyourguide-commission-rates/), [Arival](https://arival.travel/article/getyourguide-commission-increasing-for-some-operators/)). Klook's S-1 shows a 17.8% take rate on $3.04B GTV ([Mostly Metrics](https://www.mostlymetrics.com/p/klook-ipo-s1-breakdown)). Airbnb Experiences charges a flat 20%, Services 15% ([Arival](https://arival.travel/article/inside-airbnb-experiences-relaunch/)). Fever takes 10 to 25% ([Vizologi](https://vizologi.com/business-strategy-canvas/fever-business-model-canvas/)). Hotel activity desks and concierges expect 15 to 20%, wholesalers and receptives 25 to 30% ([Junglebee](https://junglebee.com/blog/set-tour-net-rates-without-killing-profit)). Booking software is much cheaper: FareHarbor 6 to 8% passed to the guest, Peek 6 to 8%, Rezdy and Checkfront about $99 a month plus 3%, Xola 2.39% + $0.30, Bookeo $15 to $120 a month flat ([CaptainBook](https://www.captainbook.io/blog/fareharbor-pricing-fees-explained), [Bókun pricing guide](https://www.bokun.io/tour-and-activity-booking-system-pricing)). The market has therefore settled on two price points: 20 to 30% for demand, 3 to 8% for software. Outset can sit between them.

**Average order value.** Derived: Viator's $4.7B over 22.9M bookings is **about $205 per booking**. GetYourGuide's 18 million experiences in H1 2026 against €1B+ annual revenue at a 25 to 30% take implies a similar $150 to 200 range. Outset's categories run higher than city walking tours: a two-person jet ski hour is $150 to 250, a tandem skydive $250 to 350, a half-day charter $600 to 1,200, a helicopter tour $200 to 400 a seat. Expect Outset AOV of **$250 to 400**, which matters because it makes a 10 to 15% take worth $30 to 50 a booking, enough to fund paid acquisition.

**Seasonality.** Tripadvisor states its results are "seasonally highest in the second and third quarters" driven by activities and experiences ([Tripadvisor 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1526520/000156459020051304/trip-10q_20200930.htm)); Viator's Q3 2025 GBV was $1.3B of the $4.7B year, so Q2 plus Q3 is roughly 60% of annual volume. For Outset's outdoor categories in the northern US and Canada the skew is sharper: water sports and parasailing operators frequently open Memorial Day to Labor Day and do 70 to 80% of revenue in 14 weeks. Escape rooms, axe throwing, go-karts and indoor paintball are counter-seasonal and are the winter revenue base. Lead times are short: Checkfront platform data puts about a quarter of activity bookings on the same day and about a third within a week ([Travelity summary](https://travelity.app/blog/mobile-booking-tours/)); mobile was 28% of activity bookings in 2023 per Arival, 44%+ per Checkfront. Same-day, mobile, in-destination booking is the core use case, which favors a fast listing page with real availability over a research-heavy site.

## 2. Competitors and adjacent players

### Demand marketplaces

**Viator (Tripadvisor).** $4.7B GBV, 22.9M bookings, ~400,000 experiences, 65,000+ operators, 4,000 demand partners; Experiences is 49% of Tripadvisor revenue ([Tripadvisor](https://ir.tripadvisor.com/static-files/aa7fa441-63ca-4330-b1cb-20d4a2498b8f), [AltexSoft](https://www.altexsoft.com/travel-industry-news/tripadvisor-experiences-drive-30-of-2025-profit/)). Does well: SEO scale (Tripadvisor's programmatic "things to do in" pages drive 200M+ monthly organic visits, [upGrowth](https://upgrowth.in/tripadvisor-programmatic-seo/)), reviews, affiliate network, now inside ChatGPT, Claude, Alexa+ and TikTok GO. Pricing: 20% base commission, plus "Accelerate" ad auctions where operators report effective rates of 30 to 35% and offers up to 42 to 51% to suppress competitor ads, and a $29 fee per new product since August 2025 ([Arival](https://arival.travel/article/viator-accelerate-2-0-what-it-is-and-why-it-matters/), [OTA Playbook](https://otaplaybook.com/viator-commission-rate-what-operators-actually-pay/)). Weaknesses: operator resentment of rising effective commission is at a high; onboarding requires the operator to build the listing; supply skews to city tours and attractions, not a rural kayak outfitter; no software for the operator's own direct bookings. Exploit: Outset's operator dashboard is free and its listing is pre-built, and it can sell "no auction, one flat rate" as a positioning line.

**GetYourGuide.** €1B+ revenue in 2025, profitable, 18M experiences booked in H1 2026, $194M raised with a stated "win America" priority ([GetYourGuide](https://www.getyourguide.press/blog/summer2026momentum), [Arival](https://arival.travel/article/getyourguide-adds-ai-tools-with-194-million-investment/)). Commission 25 to 30%, with a mid-2025 round of unannounced increases that operators pushed back on and in some cases reversed ([Arival](https://arival.travel/article/getyourguide-commission-increasing-for-some-operators/)). Strong brand marketing (first US national campaign), "Originals" exclusive products, creator program at up to 8%. Weakness in Outset's segment: the US supply is still thin outside top-20 metros, and it is a heavy, curated onboarding. Learn: GYG's Originals show that owning a few exclusive, photographed, well-priced products per city lifts conversion for everything else.

**Airbnb Experiences (relaunched May 2025) and Services.** 20% commission on Experiences, 15% on Services, promotional 10% rates offered to some operators; human review of every listing; Airbnb wanted "thousands of pieces of inventory" ready at launch and rejected generic experiences; hosts complained about AI-rewritten descriptions and altered times ([Arival](https://arival.travel/article/inside-airbnb-experiences-relaunch/), [Touchstay](https://touchstay.com/blog/airbnb-2025-summer-release)). By Q2 2026 Airbnb reported Experiences supply up nearly 80% year over year, but on a small base, and it is still a rounding error inside $27.2B quarterly GBV ([Airbnb Q2 2026](https://news.airbnb.com/airbnb-q2-2026-financial-results/)). Weakness: Airbnb explicitly wants "unique, host-led" experiences, which excludes most of Outset's inventory (equipment rentals, charters, go-karts, escape rooms). Exploit: Outset can be the Airbnb-shaped interface for exactly the commodity-but-fun inventory Airbnb rejects. Also copy their claim: a human-reviewed listing with a real name behind it converts better than an anonymous one.

**Klook.** $3.04B GTV, $540M revenue, 17.8% take rate, filed for NYSE IPO in November 2025 ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/ipo/2025/travel-platform-klook-books-24-growth-in-ipo-filing/), [Mostly Metrics](https://www.mostlymetrics.com/p/klook-ipo-s1-breakdown)). Asia-first; US presence is mostly inbound Asian travelers to Hawaii, LA, NYC, Vegas. Learn: Klook's low take rate and heavy attractions ticketing show that a lower take can still produce a public company at scale. Not a direct threat in Outset's categories outside Hawaii and Vegas.

**Expedia Local Expert.** Commission 15 to 35%, net-rate model, and Expedia is **no longer accepting new tour and activity suppliers directly**; new supply comes only via connectivity partners ([Bókun](https://www.bokun.io/expedia-supplier), [Xola](https://www.xola.com/articles/alternatives-to-expedia-local-expert/)). Weak on small outdoor operators. Exploit: an Outset operator can be told honestly that Expedia's door is closed to them and Outset's is open.

**Peek (Peek Pro plus Peek.com).** About 4,300 companies use Peek Pro, 3,774 in the US; $7B+ processed lifetime ([6sense](https://6sense.com/tech/reservation-online-booking/peek-pro-tour-operators-market-share), [Peek Pro](https://www.peekpro.com/)). Free tier takes 6% on direct bookings; paid from $199 a month with lower fees; Peek.com marketplace bookings are zero commission for Pro customers ([Bókun](https://www.bokun.io/peek-pro-pricing)). This is the closest structural analog to Outset: free software funded by a booking fee, plus a marketplace as a lure. Weakness: Peek.com has thin consumer traffic and is a feature of the software, not a destination; pricing is opaque and fees are among the highest ([Bókun](https://www.bokun.io/peek-pro-pricing)). Learn: Peek proved operators accept "free software, we take a cut of online bookings." Exploit: Outset ships the marketplace first and the software as a claim reward, the reverse of Peek.

**FareHarbor (Booking Holdings).** 23,000+ businesses, mostly North America; no subscription; 6 to 8% booking fee passed to the guest at checkout, 2% on API bookings, so effective 9 to 11% with card processing ([CaptainBook](https://www.captainbook.io/blog/fareharbor-pricing-fees-explained), [Arival on API fees](https://arival.travel/article/api-fees-increasing-among-booking-systems/)). September 2025: Booking.com is adding 150,000 FareHarbor products to Booking.com Attractions in 5,000+ cities, with conversational AI to navigate them ([Skift](https://skift.com/2025/09/29/booking-com-to-add-150000-attractions-with-fareharbor-tie-in/), [Booking.com](https://news.booking.com/bookingcom-further-expands-attractions-and-activities-offerings-through-new-global-integration-with-fareharbor/)). Does well: aggressive, free, white-glove onboarding (they build your listings for you), a large distribution network. Weakness: 1,400 of Outset's operators already have FareHarbor, so for them Outset is a demand channel, not software. FareHarbor is the strongest potential copier of Outset because it already has menus, prices and availability for 23,000 operators plus Booking.com's demand. Outset should treat FareHarbor as both a partner (its API is the way to get real availability for those 1,400) and the most likely competitor to launch an "AI concierge per operator."

**Xola, Rezdy, Checkfront, Bookeo.** Xola: 2.39% + $0.30 per transaction, strong for rentals and multi-resource scheduling. Rezdy: ~$99 a month + 3%, best-in-class channel manager into OTAs. Checkfront: $99 a month + 3 to 3.5%, strong for rentals and lodging bundles. Bookeo: flat $15 to $120 a month, popular with escape rooms and classes ([Bókun comparison](https://www.bokun.io/tour-and-activity-booking-system-pricing), [automate.travel](https://automate.travel/booking-engine-pricing/)). None has meaningful consumer demand. For Outset they are integration targets, not threats; each exposes availability through widgets or APIs Outset already parses for FareHarbor and Xola.

**Google Things to do and Google Maps.** Free listings; to be bookable an operator needs a Google Business Profile plus a Google-approved connectivity partner; Reserve with Google was retired for tours in 2021 ([Arival](https://arival.travel/article/5-things-operators-need-to-know-about-the-end-of-reserve-with-google/), [FareHarbor](https://fareharbor.com/blog/goodbye-reserve-with-google-hello-things-to-do/)). Google AI Mode now completes restaurant, event ticket and beauty bookings through partners (OpenTable, Resy, Ticketmaster, Booksy, Fresha, Mindbody, Appointy, Wix) and is testing hotels with Booking.com, Expedia, Marriott and others ([Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/agentic-plans-booking-travel-canvas-ai-mode/), [Skift](https://skift.com/2026/08/07/google-confirms-hotel-agentic-booking-is-now-in-testing/)). Tours and activities are not yet an AI Mode agentic vertical, but the plumbing (Things to do feeds via connectivity partners) exists. Exploit: become a Things to do connectivity partner so that every claimed Outset operator's product appears as bookable on their own Google Maps card. This is the single largest free demand source for local, same-day intent.

**Yelp.** 8.4 million active claimed local business locations (reported as 8,392 thousand) and 300M+ reviews ([Yelp 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1345016/000134501626000019/yelp-20251231.htm)). Yelp has moved into restaurant reservations, an AI phone host, and a ChatGPT app for reservations and waitlists ([Yelp blog](https://blog.yelp.com/news/yelp-chatgpt-integration/), [Yelp Host](https://blog.yelp.com/news/yelp-host-voice-ai-adds-opentable-reservations-and-takeout-ordering-for-restaurants/)). It has no activities booking. Learn from Yelp: the unclaimed-page model works at eight-figure scale, and its "your page is costing you customers" claim email is the template. Weakness: Yelp's reputation with SMBs is poor because of aggressive ad sales; Outset should promise in writing never to sell placement against an operator's own name.

**TikTok GO and Instagram.** TikTok GO launched in the US on May 12, 2026 with Booking.com, Expedia, Viator, GetYourGuide, Tiqets and Trip.com as booking partners; creators earn commissions on bookings driven from their videos ([TikTok newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/introducing-tiktok-go?lang=en), [PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/technology/tiktok-go-travel-booking-expedia-getyourguide-viator)). Instagram has no equivalent native booking for experiences; creators use link-in-bio and 8% affiliate programs from Viator and GetYourGuide ([UpPromote](https://uppromote.com/affiliate-directory/getyourguide/)). Exploit: only inventory on a TikTok GO partner is bookable in TikTok; a jet ski shop in Lake Havasu is not. Outset can offer creators a higher affiliate rate (10 to 15% of a 12 to 15% take is thin, so fund it from a creator budget in the first two metros) and deep links that actually book.

**Fever.** $724M revenue reported for 2025, ~$1.8 to 2B valuation, 100+ cities, 10 to 25% commission plus "Originals" it produces itself ([GetLatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/feverup.com), [Fever newsroom](https://newsroom.feverup.com/en-US/250714-fever-secures-100m-strengthening-its-position-as-the-leading-independent-live-entertainment-tech-platform/)). Strong at urban, ticketed, dated events and Candlelight concerts. Not in outdoor rentals or charters. Learn: Fever's email and push list per city is its moat; it built demand city by city, not nationally.

**Eventbrite.** 3.7% + $1.79 per US ticket plus 2.9% processing, new per-event organizer fees, paid ticket volume down 7% and paid creators down 5% year over year in Q2 2025 ([Eventbrite 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1475115/000147511525000115/eb-20250930.htm), [Checkoutpage](https://checkoutpage.com/blog/eventbrite-fees)). Lesson: a fee-per-ticket model that raises prices on creators without giving them demand bleeds supply. Don't do that.

### Service-marketplace analogies

**Booksy.** 140,000+ businesses, 40M consumers, $10B+ GTV, $269M raised, $84M in September 2025 ([GetLatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/booksy), [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/booksy-1/financials)). It won barbers by going deep in one community, spending its pre-seed cash on a partnership with the Instagram account barbershopconnect to enter the US, and selling a straightforward subscription ([Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/how-booksy-broke-into-america)). It became a Google AI Mode agentic booking launch partner in August 2025 ([Booksy](https://biz.booksy.com/blog/booksy-google-ai-mode-integration)).

**Fresha.** Free core software, 130,000 businesses, 35M appointments a month, $1.4B monthly volume, $285M raised, valued above $1B, monetized through payments and a 20% new-client marketplace fee ([Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/fresha/__RtZ3_FAiFOVypRalj38jh-J45D_BbHAjdaAklWTJqko), [TAMradar](https://www.tamradar.com/funding-rounds/fresha-growth-80m)). In 2026 Fresha reported **1 in 4 marketplace bookings driven by Google Gemini and AI agents, growing 50% month on month**, and says it is the first beauty marketplace to work directly with ChatGPT and Claude ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/the-ai-revolution-in-selfcare-fresha-reports-1-in-4-bookings-driven-by-google-gemini-and-ai-agents-as-marketplace-roi-hits-9x-302694734.html)). That is the clearest public evidence that "agent-ready supply" already produces bookings.

**Mindbody and ClassPass.** Mindbody is subscription software ($1B+ raised) that added a marketplace; ClassPass (acquired by Mindbody) spent $50M+ on marketing in 2025 and has paid partners $3.1B lifetime ([ClassPass](https://classpass.com/partners/blog/classpass-invested-50-million-marketing-2025-drive-partner-growth)). Mindbody has no official MCP server as of May 2026; community ones exist ([GitHub](https://github.com/NoBanks/mindbody-mcp)). Lesson: software-first companies are slow to expose booking to agents, which leaves room for a marketplace-first player.

**The common pattern.** Booksy, Fresha, Toast and DoorDash all removed the up-front cost for the small business (free software, or the platform doing the data entry), attacked one dense community or metro first, and monetized on the transaction. DoorDash's first version was a landing page with PDF menus from eight Palo Alto restaurants and the founders' phone numbers; they placed orders by phone on the restaurant's behalf and had done only 217 deliveries when YC funded them ([Vator](https://vator.tv/2019-01-04-when-doordash-was-young-the-early-years/), [Startup Archive](https://www.startuparchive.org/p/stanley-tang-shares-three-lessons-from-the-founding-story-of-doordash)). That is exactly Outset's situation with phone-only operators: list them, take the booking, relay it by phone, and let the software follow.

## 3. Distribution

**How incumbents get supply.** Viator and GetYourGuide use inbound supplier portals plus market managers in top destinations, and both now pull inventory through connectivity (FareHarbor, Bókun, Rezdy, Peek). FareHarbor uses a large onboarding team that builds the account for the operator at no charge and is now the supply pipe into Booking.com. Airbnb hand-recruited and human-reviewed. Peek bundles a marketplace with software. None of them cold-onboards a phone-only kayak shop at scale, because the cost of a human-built listing is too high relative to a single operator's annual volume. Outset's crawler changes that unit economics: the listing costs cents, not a market manager's afternoon.

**How they get demand.** Tripadvisor and Viator: programmatic SEO at 200M+ monthly organic visits plus a 4,000-partner affiliate network. GetYourGuide: brand TV and performance marketing, creator program at 8%. Klook and Fever: app-first, city email lists, exclusives. All four are now embedded in ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Google AI Mode and TikTok GO, which is the new distribution layer (Section 4).

**Pre-populated "claim your listing".** The Antler marketplace playbook reports near-100% activation when suppliers are shown a pre-built listing with photos, descriptions and prices pulled from their existing channels, versus far lower activation from cold outreach that asks them to build one ([Antler](https://www.antler.co/blog/how-to-grow-marketplace-supply-fast)). Yelp and Google Business Profile ran the same play at scale: Google says about 11% of profiles remain unclaimed and roughly 82% are verified ([SQ Magazine](https://sqmagazine.co.uk/google-my-business-statistics/), [NewMedia](https://newmedia.com/blog/google-business-profile-statistics)); Yelp counts 8.4M active claimed locations. Outset's 12,400 pre-built pages are the same asset.

**What cold outreach converts at.** 2025 to 2026 B2B cold email benchmarks: average reply rate 3 to 5%, top quartile 10 to 15%, with hooks tied to a concrete event or timeline (for example "you have a booking request") outperforming problem-hooks two to one ([Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks/), [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates), [Digital Bloom](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/cold-outbound-reply-rate-benchmarks/)). For SMB owners who answer their own phone, a call after an email typically lifts contact rate to 15 to 25% but takes 5 to 8 dials per connect. Planning numbers for Outset: 12,400 emails, 5% reply (620), half of those claim (300), plus 200 from calls and the "you have a booking" trigger, so **about 500 claimed operators in the first quarter is achievable with one person on outreach**. The trigger email ("a guest wants to book Saturday at 10, accept or decline") should convert several times higher than a generic claim email; that is the DoorDash trick.

**SEO and programmatic pages.** The winning pattern is "[activity] in [place]" and "things to do in [place]" pages generated from structured data, but Google in 2026 treats template-only pages as thin; pages need real enrichment (unique photos, prices, hours, an assistant answer block, reviews) to rank ([Firestorm](https://firestorm-internet.com/insights/programmatic-seo-travel/), [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/programmatic-seo-real-world-examples-insights-433490)). Outset has the enrichment already: per-operator menus, prices and photos are precisely what Viator's category pages lack for small outdoor operators. Target long-tail queries where Viator has zero or one result ("jet ski rental Lake Havasu", "axe throwing Kelowna"), which are also where Google's own Things to do module is empty.

**TikTok and Instagram creators.** TikTok GO pays creators commission only on partner-bookable inventory; Viator and GetYourGuide pay 8%. Outset's edge is that its inventory is not on those partners, so a creator filming a parasail in Destin has no bookable link today. Offer 10% plus a fixed $50 per video in the launch metros, seeded with 20 to 30 micro-creators (10k to 100k followers) at roughly $200 to 500 per post. Expect $5 to 15 cost per booking on good posts, and many posts that produce nothing.

**Google Maps.** Most Outset operators have a Google Business Profile with no booking link. Becoming a Things to do connectivity partner puts an Outset "Book" link on the card; until then, the claim flow should ask operators to paste their Outset link into the Google Business Profile "appointment URL" field, which is free and immediate.

**Hotels, concierges, short-term-rental hosts.** Activity desks expect 15 to 20% ([Junglebee](https://junglebee.com/blog/set-tour-net-rates-without-killing-profit)); TripShock, a Gulf Coast activities marketplace, runs a formal affiliate program for property managers and vacation rentals ([TripShock Partners](https://partners.tripshock.com/elevating-experiences-for-property-managers-and-hotels-the-expansion-of-our-affiliate-program-to-the-tripshock-activity-concierge-service/)). Airbnb hosts in a beach metro are the cheapest version: a QR code in the welcome book with a 10% host referral. This is a real channel but slow and manual; use it in the two launch metros only.

**Paid.** Google search CPC for travel averages $1.34 to $2.12; long-tail experience queries often under $1.50; Meta travel CPC around $0.63 and CPM $12 to 15 ([WordStream](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/2025-google-ads-benchmarks), [PPC Chief](https://ppcchief.com/google-ads-cost/travel), [SuperAds](https://www.superads.ai/facebook-ads-costs/cpm-cost-per-mille/recreation-and-travel)). At a 2.5% landing conversion ([Obvlo](https://obvlo.com/resources/travel-website-conversion-benchmarks/)) a $1.50 click is a $60 booking, which a $300 AOV at 12% take ($36) does not cover. Paid search only pays on high-AOV categories (charters, skydiving, helicopters) or at a higher take. Meta at $0.63 a click is marginal. Paid should be a small test budget, not the plan.

## 4. AI-native opportunities

**What changed in 2025 to 2026.**
- OpenAI shipped apps in ChatGPT (October 2025) with Expedia, Booking.com and Tripadvisor as launch partners, then in March 2026 pulled Instant Checkout out of the main interface and handed transactions to partner apps via the Agentic Commerce Protocol; Expedia rose 12% and Booking 8% on the news ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-apps-in-chatgpt/), [Skift](https://skift.com/2026/03/05/openai-chatgpt-checkout-walkback/), [PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/technology/openai-chatgpt-instant-checkout-travel-intermediaries)). Read this correctly: the assistant does discovery, the marketplace does the transaction. The marketplace with the cleanest app wins the handoff.
- Google launched agentic bookings in AI Mode for restaurants, event tickets and beauty appointments (August 2025, expanded November 2025 and to eight countries in April 2026), is testing hotels (August 2026), and published the Universal Commerce Protocol in January 2026, compatible with MCP, A2A and AP2 ([Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/agentic-plans-booking-travel-canvas-ai-mode/), [Google Developers](https://developers.google.com/merchant/ucp), [Winbuzzer](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/04/13/google-ai-mode-rolls-out-restaurant-booking-to-8-countries-xcxwbn/)).
- Perplexity books hotels natively via Selfbook with Tripadvisor supplying content and Viator's experiences ([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/perplexity-selfbook-agentic-ai-travel-booking-tripadvisor)).
- Tripadvisor and Viator are in ChatGPT, Claude and Alexa+; Claude users can pick Viator as a "travel partner" and get hand-off to book ([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/technology/tripadvisor-viator-claude-anthropic-amazon-alexa-ai)).
- Fresha reports 25% of marketplace bookings now come from Gemini and AI agents, up 50% month on month ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/the-ai-revolution-in-selfcare-fresha-reports-1-in-4-bookings-driven-by-google-gemini-and-ai-agents-as-marketplace-roi-hits-9x-302694734.html)).
- Yelp put reservations and waitlist inside ChatGPT and runs an AI phone host for restaurants ([Yelp](https://blog.yelp.com/news/yelp-chatgpt-integration/)).

**What an agent-ready listing needs.** An agent cannot book from a PDF menu or a "call for pricing" page. It needs: (1) structured products with price, duration, capacity and age or weight limits; (2) real or near-real availability by slot; (3) explicit cancellation, deposit and weather policies; (4) a deterministic quote and hold endpoint; (5) a payment path (Stripe, AP2 or ACP tokens); (6) an identity the agent can trust (verified operator, response SLA). Schema.org `Product`/`Offer`/`Event` markup gets you found; an MCP server or UCP checkout gets you booked. Almost none of Outset's 10,900 phone-only operators has any of this today, and the OTAs only have it for operators who did the work in a supplier portal.

**Who already exposes booking to agents.** OpenTable, Resy, Tock, Ticketmaster, StubBook, SeatGeek, Vivid Seats, Booksy, Fresha, Mindbody, Appointy, Wix (Google AI Mode); Expedia, Booking.com, Tripadvisor, Skyscanner, Accor (ChatGPT apps); Selfbook via Perplexity. For tours and activities specifically, only the OTAs and their connected inventory. **No one has agent-ready availability for a small-town charter, parasail or go-kart track.** That is the open lane.

**Concrete ideas for Outset.**
1. *Otto as the operator's agent, not just a chatbot.* Give each listing an MCP server and a UCP-style checkout so that ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Perplexity can quote and hold a slot. Ship one MCP server for all of Outset with a per-operator tool namespace; register it in the OpenAI app directory and with Anthropic's connectors. Fresha's 25% number says this can be a top-three channel within a year.
2. *Guest concierge.* A metro-level Otto ("what can four people do in Kelowna on Saturday under $400") that only answers from Outset inventory and ends in a booking. This is the ChatGPT app surface; it is also the SEO answer block on programmatic pages.
3. *Auto-generated listings as the product.* The crawl plus extraction pipeline is already the listing factory. Expose it to operators as "paste your website, get a bookable menu in 60 seconds" so that operators outside the crawl self-onboard, and as a re-sync so prices stay current.
4. *Voice for phone-only operators.* Outset should answer the phone. A per-operator voice agent (Twilio plus a realtime model) that reads from the same Otto data, takes a booking request, and texts the operator to accept or decline mirrors Yelp Host and fixes the 60% missed-call problem. This is the feature that turns "we take bookings by phone" from an obstacle into the wedge: the operator keeps their phone number and workflow and gains a receptionist. Price it inside the take rate.
5. *Agentic confirmation.* For unclaimed operators, Outset's own agent calls or texts the operator with the request, gets a yes or no, and confirms the guest. This is the DoorDash "we phone the order in" bridge, automated.
6. *Structured policy extraction.* The crawler should pull deposit, cancellation, weather, weight and age rules into fields, because these are the questions agents and guests ask most and the ones operators answer by phone today.

## 5. Unfair advantage and moats

**What Outset has that nobody else has.** A near-complete structured map of small outdoor and indoor experience operators in the US and Canada with menus, prices, photos, videos, policies and hours, with an assistant grounded on each one. Viator has 65,000 operators globally but they are the ones who chose to fill in a portal; GetYourGuide is thinner in North America; FareHarbor knows its 23,000 customers and nobody else; Google has the map pin and the phone number but not the menu. In the segment of phone-only outdoor operators, Outset's catalog is plausibly the most complete structured dataset in existence.

**Honest assessment of defensibility.**
- *The crawl itself is not a moat.* Anyone with $50 of extraction budget and Overpass can rebuild it in a month; FareHarbor and Google could do it in a week. Data that was public when you copied it is not defensible.
- *Claimed and corrected operators are a moat.* Each operator who claims, edits prices, uploads photos and starts accepting requests is proprietary, current data plus a relationship. Booksy's and Fresha's value is not their directories, it is 130,000 businesses whose calendars live there. Every claimed operator raises the cost for a copier.
- *Availability is the real moat.* The OTAs' weakness in this segment is that they cannot see a jet ski shop's calendar. If Outset becomes the calendar for phone-only operators (via the dashboard and the voice agent), it holds the one thing agents and OTAs need and cannot crawl.
- *Reviews and booking history compound.* Verified bookings produce verified reviews, which produce conversion, which produce more bookings. Viator's reviews are its most durable asset.
- *Agent distribution is first-mover but not exclusive.* Being in the OpenAI, Anthropic and Google directories for "book outdoor activities in North America" before FareHarbor or Viator pays off for a year or two, not forever.

**What a strong competitor would do.** FareHarbor plus Booking.com is the most dangerous: it already has structured products and availability for 23,000 operators, a Booking.com AI concierge, and free onboarding staff. Its move would be "FareHarbor Lite": a free, crawler-built listing with a request-to-book flow for non-customers, feeding Booking.com Attractions. Viator's move would be to waive the $29 product fee and auto-build listings from websites for US outdoor categories. Google's move would be to extend AI Mode agentic booking to Things to do with a "request via phone" fallback using Duplex, which it has the technology for. None has done this as of September 2026, largely because each is organized around operators who self-serve.

**Where to be first.**
1. Voice-plus-dashboard for phone-only operators (no one does this for activities).
2. Agent-bookable availability for the long tail (MCP and UCP for small outdoor operators).
3. Per-item photos and videos and a grounded assistant on every listing, the Uber Eats treatment for a category that still sells "tandem" and "mega board" as text.
4. Two or three dense metros where Outset has 80%+ of local operators claimed, so that guests and creators find everything in one place. Density in a metro is worth more than 12,400 thin national listings.

### The claim-your-listing playbook

Yelp and Google made claiming free and framed it as a fix ("your page is missing hours and photos; customers are seeing wrong info"); Zillow uses landlords' listings to prompt claims and then sells tools. Booksy and Fresha did not seed unclaimed pages; they sold free or cheap software into one community at a time and let the marketplace follow. Outset's version should combine both:

1. *Subject line that names the thing*: "Your [Business] listing on Outset has 3 questions from guests" or "A guest wants to book [Product] on Saturday." Timeline and event hooks reply at about 10% versus 4% for problem hooks ([Digital Bloom](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/cold-outbound-reply-rate-benchmarks/)).
2. *One click, no password*: the claim link logs them straight into the dashboard (already built). Show their own menu, prices and photos; ask them to fix one thing.
3. *Three things they get*: their bookings feed, Otto answering questions from their own site, and a phone line that never misses a call. Do not lead with commission.
4. *Three sends over 10 days, then a call.* Call only in launch metros. Track claims per send by category and metro; kill what does not move.
5. *Opt-out honored instantly and listing removal honored within 24 hours.* This is both a legal requirement and the fastest way to defuse the angry-operator post on a Facebook group, which is where operators talk.

### Legal notes (not legal advice; get a lawyer before the outreach batch)

- *Listing a business without consent* is generally lawful in the US when it uses factual public information (name, address, phone, hours, prices), which is what Yelp and Google do. Do not use the operator's logo as if Outset were the operator, do not imply endorsement, and label unclaimed pages as such internally while keeping the guest UI clean.
- *Photos and videos copied from operator websites are copyrighted by the operator or the photographer they hired.* Zillow is defending a CoStar suit over about 47,000 (later 53,000) photos it displayed to prompt landlords to claim properties, with damages claimed above $1 billion, and CoStar alleges Zillow re-displayed images after takedown ([CoStar](https://www.costargroup.com/press-room/2025/costar-sues-zillow-rampant-copyright-infringement), [Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2026/03/27/costar-claims-zillow-is-still-using-over-50k-copyrighted-photos)). Outset's exposure is smaller (the copyright holder is usually the operator being promoted, not a hostile rival), but the mechanics are identical. Mitigations: register a DMCA agent with the Copyright Office and post the policy ([Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/512/)); remove on request within 24 hours and log it so images never reappear; prefer photos where the operator is the obvious source; replace crawled photos with operator uploads on claim; and treat professional or stock imagery detected in the crawl as higher risk. Safe harbor is weaker when the platform itself selected and uploaded the content, so the takedown process, not safe harbor, is the real protection.
- *Outreach law.* US CAN-SPAM is opt-out: a cold email to a business is legal with an accurate header, a physical address and a working unsubscribe. **Canada's CASL is opt-in with no B2B exemption**; a cold commercial email to a Canadian operator needs express or implied consent (a conspicuously published address with no "no solicitation" notice gives implied consent for relevant messages), and 2025 settlements ran $5,000 to $250,000 for ordinary SMB senders with 152,603 complaints filed in H1 2025 ([ANA](https://www.ana.net/content/show/id/accountability-digital-marketing-CASL), [Smarte](https://www.smarte.pro/blog/casl-compliance)). For Canada: rely on the published-address implied consent, keep the message relevant to their business, include identification and unsubscribe, and prefer a phone call or a booking-triggered transactional message, which is not a commercial electronic message. Do not text Canadian operators cold.
- *Payments and bookings on behalf of unclaimed operators.* If Outset charges a guest for an unclaimed operator's service, it is the merchant of record and owns the refund if the operator says no. Keep unclaimed bookings as requests with card authorization only, captured on operator accept.

### 90-day go-to-market, two metros

Pick metros with dense outdoor and indoor inventory, year-round or shoulder demand, and weak OTA coverage in Outset's categories. Good candidates from the catalog's categories: **Florida Gulf Coast (Destin to Clearwater)** or **Lake Havasu and Phoenix** for water sports and airborne; **Denver and Colorado Springs** or **Vancouver and Kelowna** for a mixed set with escape rooms and axe throwing in winter. Choose one US and one Canadian metro to learn both legal regimes.

- *Days 1 to 15*: pick metros; hand-QA the top 150 listings in each (photos, prices, hours, policies); ship Otto phone answering and the booking-trigger SMS; register the DMCA agent; set up outreach tooling and CASL-compliant Canadian templates.
- *Days 16 to 45*: run the claim sequence to every operator in both metros (1,000 to 1,500 operators); call the top 100 by rating in each; seed 20 creators per metro; publish programmatic "[activity] in [metro]" pages; ask claimed operators to set their GBP appointment link. Target: 150 claimed operators, 50 first bookings.
- *Days 46 to 90*: expand the sequence to the remaining national list in batches by state; ship the MCP server and apply for OpenAI app directory listing; approach FareHarbor for a distribution-network partnership covering the 1,400 FareHarbor operators; start the Things to do connectivity application; run a $3 to 5k paid test on charters and skydiving. Target: 400 to 500 claimed operators nationally, 300 to 500 bookings, 10+ operators with the voice agent live, and one publishable stat ("Outset answered 2,000 calls operators would have missed").

Measure: claims per 100 emails by category, request-to-accept rate and time, booking conversion on listing pages, share of bookings from unclaimed operators, calls answered by Otto, and take per booking.
